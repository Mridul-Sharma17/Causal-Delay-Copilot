## 1. Executive Recommendation

**Causal Delay Copilot enters the prototype round as one deeply built decision workflow: when a construction material or equipment order is flagged as at-risk, the system investigates whether *supplier congestion* — the order having been placed while its supplier was carrying an unusually high concurrent open-order load relative to that supplier's own history — is supported under stated assumptions as a driver of supplier-controlled milestone slippage, quantifies the effect in days with honest uncertainty and a separate Robustness Grade, and walks the manager from that evidence to a separately governed, human-authorized decision.**

The central causal question, in the one sentence a judge should be able to repeat:

> **"Among comparable exposed orders, did unusually high supplier load increase average supplier-controlled slippage under the stated assumptions, and does that population evidence apply to this order?"**

The managerial decision being improved is the response decision under a risk flag. Today a project manager sees "delay likely" and everything afterwards is guesswork under time pressure. The Copilot replaces that guesswork with an inspectable causal case — an effect estimate in days when permitted, a confidence interval, the adjustment set, a hidden-confounding Robustness Grade, a refutation scoreboard — and, only when the Evidence Verdict permits, lets separate Decision Support produce a draft action artefact (capacity-slot request, alternate-vendor inquiry, re-sequencing note) that the manager approves, edits, or rejects. When the evidence does not support a causal conclusion, the system says so and abstains. The same engine also runs proactively as a pre-award check, one step earlier in the same workflow.

Why this is the strongest direction:

1. **It is the best-evidenced candidate.** The exposure–outcome family (congested/shared supplier → delivery delay) carries the only peer-reviewed causal machine-learning estimate in supply-chain risk management: Wyrembek, Baryannis & Brintrup (2025), *International Journal of Production Research* 63(15), 5629–5648, DOI 10.1080/00207543.2025.2458121, which used Double Machine Learning on 77,526 real engineer-to-order maritime orders and estimated roughly +16.7 days of average delay for shared-supplier exposure. **[Verified — journal page and full text inspected]** Independent, construction-adjacent support arrived in January 2026: a Samsung Heavy Industries shipyard study (Lee et al., arXiv:2601.19296) whose own conclusion recommends balancing workload across fabrication vendors to prevent excessive concentration — and whose limitations concede that its prediction accuracy gains were never tested against decisions or operating costs. **[Verified — paper inspected; it cites Wyrembek et al. as its first reference]**
2. **It is empirically demonstrable with an honest three-layer data strategy** in which every dataset does only the job it is qualified for: a fully disclosed semi-synthetic construction dataset with known ground truth carries the narrative and proves the validity machinery; the real Olist order dataset (99,441 orders, 3,095 sellers, audited this cycle) proves the identical pipeline runs on genuine operational data; and the USAID SCMS dataset — which this cycle's audit **disqualified** as a causal source — becomes a live demonstration that the system refuses to estimate on invalid data.
3. **It preserves Stage 1 continuity exactly** — same name, track, users, and causal decision-support proposition — while repairing Stage 1's scientifically weak claims: "true cause" language is removed product-wide, the static shared-supplier flag is upgraded to a time-varying within-supplier exposure, and the unexplained "+18.5%" effect visual is replaced by days, intervals, and provenance.
4. **It is the candidate where causality visibly changes the decision.** Congestion is observable before commitment; the interventions map onto the exposure; and the evaluation harness can show, against prediction-only and correlation-only baselines on known ground truth, that the causal layer selects measurably better actions — including refusing to act on a planted spurious correlate that fools SHAP.

Per the team's explicit instruction, **no decision in this report is driven by any deadline**. The verified prototype-round deliverable is a working prototype and live demo scored by the Kaya AI internal team, with finalists presenting live in Bengaluru before an executive panel; the format facts inform the design (fully local execution, two-audience demo), the calendar does not. **[Verified — official hackathon resources page]**

## 5. State of Research and Industry Evidence

Organized around the five decisions the prototype depends on.

**Decision 1 — Is the problem real and large enough? Yes, quoted precisely.** ~98% of megaprojects overrun cost by >30% and 77% run at least 40% late (McKinsey, *The Construction Productivity Imperative*); Flyvbjerg's long-run megaproject data ("iron law": nine of ten projects overrun, stable across decades and geographies, arXiv:1409.0003) is the academic backstop. Late material delivery recurs among top delay causes in cross-country construction-delay syntheses. The hackathon's own Track 2 framing describes exactly the workflow gap: teams learn *that* something is delayed, not *why* or *what to do*. **[Verified — hackathon brief]**

**Decision 2 — Does causal ML beyond prediction have credible support in this problem class? Yes, directly.** Wyrembek, Baryannis & Brintrup (2025) is, per its own review, the first causal-ML application in supply-chain risk management: practitioner DAGs cross-checked with automated discovery; DML estimating ≈+16.7 days for shared-supplier exposure on 77,526 orders; seasonal ATEs; CATE curves rising with order quantity; and policy trees for sourcing rules (Athey & Wager 2021 method). Its stated limits — single sector, mechanism unidentified, case-study generalizability — define what our prototype must handle with humility. **[Verified — full text]** On the prediction side, Lee et al. (2026, arXiv:2601.19296; Pusan National University with Samsung Heavy Industries co-authors; 106,403 pipe spools, ~2.92M procurement events) achieves 22.6–50.4% MAE reductions but its limitations state the study evaluates statistical accuracy, not operational cost savings, schedule quality, delay penalties, or expediting costs — the precise gap Causal Delay Copilot occupies. Its conclusion independently recommends balancing workload across fabrication vendors to prevent excessive concentration: construction-adjacent support for congestion as the mechanism worth acting on. **[Verified — full text]** Careful wording: the authors *did not test* the decision link; do not say they "proved prediction fails."

**Decision 3 — Is there construction-domain causal work to position against? Yes — and it sharpens, not kills, the originality claim.** A 2026 *Journal of Management in Engineering* paper (DOI 10.1061/JMENEA.MEENG-7378) presents an AI-based real-time *causal-attribution* method for construction supply-chain logistics disruptions — an LLM fine-tuned on Chinese logistics accident reports to classify direct and indirect causes (F1 0.654 / 0.525). **[Verified — publisher page]** This is diagnosis-by-classification, not treatment-effect estimation, but its existence makes any broad "first causal AI in construction supply chains" claim indefensible. The defensible originality sentence, disclosure included, appears in §19. Adjacent 2025–2026 causal-ML applications (supply-chain-finance payment delays via DoubleML; causal discovery for delivery-risk attribution) show the method family diffusing quickly — the window is real but closing, which is itself a good judge-facing line.

**Decision 4 — Is there real public data that can carry the analysis? Yes — but not the dataset conventional wisdom would pick.** This cycle's audits produced a decision-critical reversal:
- **SCMS (USAID Supply Chain Shipment Pricing / Delivery History, data.usaid.gov id a3rc-nmf6): rejected as a causal source.** The audited file has 10,324 rows and 73 vendors; only 4,592 rows (44.5%) carry a usable, distinct PO-sent date (5,404 are "N/A — From RDC" and 328 "Date Not Captured"), and every usable row is a single fulfilment channel (Direct Drop). Decisively, **89.2% of the usable subset has scheduled-delivery identical to delivered-to-client** (61.3% across the full file; 53.2% have the full scheduled/delivered/recorded triplet identical), leaving only ~5.6% of usable rows with positive delay. The scheduled-date field behaves like an administratively updated or backfilled value, not a frozen promise — so "delay vs schedule" is not a valid outcome measurement here. **[Derived audit — full CSV, 24 July 2026]** SCMS is retained only as a live abstention/data-quality vignette.
- **Olist (Brazilian e-commerce public dataset): qualified as the real-data validation layer.** Audited directly: 99,441 orders, 112,650 item rows, 3,095 sellers, 100,010 seller-order lines; 99.0% have purchase timestamp, `shipping_limit_date` (the promised supplier milestone) and `order_delivered_carrier_date` (the actual supplier milestone); supplier-controlled slippage is a real continuous outcome (sd ≈ 6.0 days; 9.0% late handoffs; tails to ~117 days); the concurrent-load exposure varies within sellers (mean 3.9, sd 8.1, max 142); among the 426 sellers with ≥50 usable lines (74,939 lines), a top-tertile-within-seller split yields **20,053 treated vs 54,886 untreated**, and the pre-adjustment gradient already points the right way: late-handoff rate **12.8% (treated) vs 7.1% (untreated)**, mean slippage gap ≈ +0.6 days. There is a genuine signal for DML to interrogate rather than manufacture. 97.4% of lines are single-seller orders (the multi-seller carrier timestamp ambiguity is handled by restricting the primary analysis to single-seller orders). **[Derived audit — full files, 24 July 2026]** Caveats handled in §11: B2C e-commerce domain, CC BY-NC-SA license.

**Decision 5 — What does Kaya actually claim, and what positioning is safe?** Verified from Kaya's own releases and the official hackathon pages: Amber is "a domain-trained AI knowledge worker built specifically for construction" that unifies drawings, specs, submittals, RFIs, supplier quotes, schedules, equipment lead times and OFCI/OFE data into a project graph; the MCP Planner™ (Mission-Critical Procurement Planner, construction-tuned small language model) launched Dec 2025; Jarvis is their project-management agent that "helps customers identify schedule risk sooner"; the company self-describes as "the predictive supply-chain intelligence platform for mission-critical construction" and reports ~90% lead-time-accuracy and ~80% procurement-time improvements (company-reported). The hackathon officially asks teams to complement or extend Amber, and offers platform access on request. **[Verified]** The team later confirmed that platform and sample-data access will not be provided for this effort; that constrains implementation and evidence claims but does not change the verified public positioning. **[Team-confirmed project constraint, 31 July 2026]** "PALS" and a "Mission-Critical Lead Time Index" were not found in any official or press source and are banned from the deck. Safe positioning sentence set appears in §18; the unsafe list includes any claim that Kaya "only predicts" or lacks explanations.

## 7. Candidate Flagship Cases (Phase A — Divergent Exploration)

All candidates remain inside construction supply-chain delay management and the fixed product identity.

**C1 — Supplier congestion (time-varying concurrent load) → supplier-controlled milestone slippage.** *Decision:* pre-award sourcing/slot choice; post-flag keep-and-expedite vs reroute-remaining vs re-sequence vs monitor. *Support:* the only candidate with a peer-reviewed causal estimate for its exposure family **[Analogous]**, plus this cycle's audited real-data instantiation (Olist) and an independent construction-adjacent mechanism endorsement (Lee et al.). *Strengths:* exact Stage 1 continuity; within-supplier identification; real pre-adjustment signal; legible in one sentence. *Weaknesses:* residual confounding from calendar-demand shocks and supplier selection; interference among orders sharing a supplier; exposure is a state, not directly an action.

**C2 — Submittal/approval latency before fabrication release → delay.** Deeply construction-native and squarely on Amber's home turf. *Weaknesses:* approval duration is mediator-rich and time-varying-confounded (complex packages both approve slower and fabricate slower); **no public event-level dataset with submittal chains and downstream supplier milestones exists** [Verified absence]; a fully synthetic hero would be exactly the "recovering our own simulation" risk the data strategy is built to avoid. Retained as the natural *second driver* and a Future extension.

**C3 — Order-timing / seasonal placement → delay.** Estimated in the anchor study (seasonal ATEs −18.7…+10.1 days) **[Analogous]** and identifiable on Olist. *Weaknesses:* a live project cannot move its schedule to another season — weak flag-time actionability; entangled with weather and demand cycles.

**C4 — Logistics/fulfilment mode choice → delay.** Directly present in shipment data. *Weaknesses:* mode is chosen *in response to* urgency (severe confounding by indication); downstream of the risk signal; addresses transport, not the supplier-capacity mechanism.

**C5 — Lead-time buffer discipline → late-to-site.** Recognizable to managers. *Weaknesses:* promised lead times may be strategically padded and are jointly determined with complexity; no public data; no published causal estimate [Verified absence].

**C6 — PO/payment latency → supplier slippage.** *Weaknesses:* proprietary and sensitive data; severe endogeneity around disputes and supplier distress; drifts toward Track 3 procurement.

**C7 — Single- vs dual-sourcing.** *Weaknesses:* firms dual-source *because* items are risky (deep endogeneity); no observed sourcing-policy interventions in any available dataset.

## 9. Selected Flagship Case — Complete Definition

**Construction context.** A mid-to-large building, data-center, or infrastructure project procures long-lead fabricated materials and equipment — structural steel assemblies, switchgear, transformers, precast elements, MEP equipment, façade units — from a vendor base that also serves other active projects; the norm in regional construction markets and precisely the mission-critical-equipment world Kaya's own materials describe. **[Inference, consistent with the ETO evidence and Kaya's verified positioning]**

**Primary user.** The project manager and the supply-chain manager, at two moments of one workflow: (1) *reactive* — a generic upstream risk event, produced in Core by the prototype's own stand-in model, flags an open order as at-risk; (2) *proactive* — at supplier award or order release, as a pre-commitment check.

**Managerial problem.** A red flag with no supported driver and no costed options. A busy supplier may still be the best supplier — capability attracts load. The manager's real question is not only "will it be late?" but "does population evidence support an actionable driver for comparable orders, does that evidence apply here, how large and robust is the estimated effect, and which separately evaluated action do I authorize?"

**Unit of analysis.** The purchase-order line (or supplier-controlled fabrication package where lines share a production slot), consistent with the anchor study's unit. **[Verified precedent]**

**Treatment / exposure.** **High supplier concurrent load at order placement**: at the order's commitment/approval time, count the supplier's previously placed orders that have not yet reached the supplier-completion/handoff milestone; classify the order as *exposed* when that count exceeds the supplier's own **expanding prior-history 67th percentile**, subject to a minimum-history requirement (≥10 prior orders), with the continuous load index retained for a continuous-load linear-slope sensitivity and threshold-sensitivity checks (75th percentile and alternative minimum-history variants pre-registered). This deliberately upgrades Stage 1's static "shared supplier" flag on three grounds: it varies *within* supplier (controlling stable supplier quality by construction); the expanding-history threshold is leakage-safe (it uses only information available at placement); and it survives the well-defined-intervention objection when framed honestly — the manager does not set the supplier's queue; the manipulable choice is **exposure choice** (supplier selection, order timing, capacity reservation, lot splitting), exactly the sourcing-policy reading of the anchor study. A composite "capacity stress index" was rejected: different ingredient combinations can produce the same score (a consistency violation) and no manager can manipulate an index. **[Recommendation, empirically grounded in the Olist audit]**

**Outcome.** **Supplier-controlled milestone slippage, in days**: actual supplier-completion/carrier-handoff (construction analogue: fabrication-release or promised ship-by milestone) minus the promised milestone **frozen at commitment**, with a binary late-handoff variant as the robustness outcome. Total delivery delay vs the customer-facing promise is retained only as a descriptive secondary, because transport, customs, and site readiness lie outside the supplier's control. On Olist this is `order_delivered_carrier_date − shipping_limit_date`, derivable on 99.0% of lines **[Derived audit]**; a system-generated shipping limit even strengthens the measurement, since slippage past it is unambiguously seller-side handling time. If the promised milestone is revised after workload becomes known, the revision is post-treatment: the data model preserves both the original commitment and revisions, and analyses use the original.

**Observed confounders (adjustment set — pre-treatment only).** Material/equipment category and complexity proxy; order quantity and value; requested lead time; season/quarter and calendar-demand controls (month, holiday); project identity/phase and urgency known before award; geography; supplier stable characteristics computed *only* from pre-placement history (baseline volume, tenure, historical on-time rate); contract form where recorded.

**Explicitly excluded (post-treatment / colliders / leakage):** production progress updates, escalation-email counts, expedite status initiated after risk emerged, revised promised dates, premium freight chosen after slippage, recovery-plan flags. The eligibility gate enforces this mechanically.

**Potential unobserved confounders.** Supplier internal capacity shocks (labour, breakdowns, financial distress); private urgency signalling and prioritization agreements; strategic promise-setting; market-wide shortages. These are the targets of the sensitivity analysis, not of denial.

**Causal estimand.** Primary: the **average treatment effect among exposed (high-load) orders (ATTE)** on slippage days — the managerially relevant population quantity for comparable orders that were placed under high load — under conditional exchangeability given the adjustment set, with supplier-clustered inference. Context: an overlap-trimmed ATE. Core reports population evidence plus subject applicability; subgroup and order-level CATEs are Stretch and require a future heterogeneity and language contract. Rationale for ATTE-primary: the flagged order is by construction an exposed order, and individualized causal estimates are unreliable at moderate sample sizes.

**Operational significance.** Only a Supported under stated assumptions verdict within a claim scope that permits Decision Support can carry the estimated load effect into separate option evaluation. It does not establish an intervention benefit: any claim that a different supplier, protected slot, timing change, or lot split may improve the milestone remains a separately evaluated, declared assumption unless that intervention was itself estimated. Tentative evidence routes to investigation; Association Only and Insufficient prohibit a driver-linked recommendation. No construction effect size is asserted; the prototype estimates its own on its own authorized data and says so.

**Available actions and linkage.** *Pre-award:* choose another qualified supplier; secure a protected production slot; split the package; adjust release timing; negotiate capacity-backed milestones. *Post-flag:* obtain a factory-slot commitment; reroute remaining/undelivered quantity; arrange phased delivery; re-sequence dependent site work; escalate contractually; accept-and-monitor (the abstention-adjacent action). Each card states whether its benefit is estimated, mechanistically linked, rule-based, or assumption-based (§13). The signature driver-logic rule: **when capacity is the binding constraint, a generic expedite request asks a congested supplier to go faster — it is suppressed unless a real acceleration mechanism exists.**

**Success condition.** A judge watching the demo can answer: what drove the risk, how big, how certain, what was adjusted for, what could break the conclusion, which action was recommended and why, where the human stayed in charge — and can see, on known ground truth, the causal layer beat prediction-only and correlation-only baselines on decision quality while refusing to act on a planted spurious correlate.

## 10. End-to-End Product Vision

One complete decision experience, described by required behaviour rather than screens.

**1 · Risk intake (reactive trigger).** The Copilot consumes a generic local risk event — order identity, risk score, timeline snapshot, supplier and equipment context — produced by its own deliberately ordinary predictive model standing in for any upstream platform. Its shape follows only the publicly described workflow and is not a tested Kaya contract. The upstream flag is treated as *untrusted input to investigate*, never as truth. The **proactive trigger** invokes the identical engine at award/release time ("you are about to place this order during a load spike at this supplier"); nothing forks — both triggers query the same estimand, and the proactive check is simply the same investigation run one step earlier.

**2 · Context freeze and eligibility gate.** The system freezes the decision timestamp and assembles only information available at that time; then checks: usable exposure measurement and sufficient supplier history; frozen promised milestone; correct temporal ordering; overlap for this order's profile; missingness profile; distribution support. Failure at this gate produces a named-trigger abstention *before* any estimate exists — bad timestamps never reach the estimator (this is the SCMS lesson, productized).

**3 · Causal investigation.** For the congestion driver: the ATTE in days with a confidence interval (supplier-clustered); the trimmed-ATE context; the adjustment set actually used; the four-refuter scoreboard (placebo treatment, dummy outcome, random common cause, and grouped-subset stability) plus the separate negative-control-outcome result; overlap and balance diagnostics; and the hidden-confounding **Robustness Grade** (*Strong / Moderate / Weak / Unavailable*, benchmarked against observed confounders — §12). Every conclusion receives one of four separate Evidence Verdicts: *Supported under stated assumptions / Tentative — fragile / Association only / Insufficient evidence — abstain*. Individualized CATE display remains Stretch and is prohibited in Core.

**4 · Transparent explanation.** A verdict-first evidence card: one plain-language verdict line and separate Robustness Grade on top (for example, "**Tentative — fragile:** evidence suggests a possible increase, but it is fragile because the threshold variants disagree materially"), with progressive disclosure one tap deep per layer: effect and interval → target population → assumptions → what was *not* observed and the confounder strength needed to overturn the result → alternative explanations considered → diagnostics. Calibrated language is enforced in generation; "true cause" cannot be rendered.

**5 · Decision support.** Only Supported under stated assumptions together with recorded claim-scope permission allows the separate intervention layer (§13) to evaluate feasible, constraint-filtered options and price their trade-offs with editable cost parameters. Out-of-domain validation never enters this layer. Tentative routes to investigation only; Association Only and Insufficient prohibit driver-linked recommendations. A Robustness Grade never authorizes Decision Support, and every resulting action remains human-authorized.

**6 · Human-authorized action.** The artefact layer drafts the email/memo strictly from the structured evidence object (§13's fencing and checker). The manager approves, edits, rejects with reason, or asks "investigate further" through a declared Core sensitivity or a new, separately evidenced and pre-registered causal question; each new run is logged, and Core offers no subgroup rerun. Nothing is sent autonomously.

**7 · Feedback and audit.** Every recommendation, its evidence snapshot, the manager's decision (including edits and disagreement reasons), and the eventual realized milestone are logged. The audit trail doubles as the future dataset for estimating *intervention* effects — the honest long-run answer to the exposure-vs-intervention gap — and replays exactly what the manager saw at decision time, a property with real value in construction's claims-and-disputes culture. **[Inference]**

**Abstention is a first-class outcome** at steps 2, 3, and 5, and the demo deliberately shows it live.

## 11. Data Strategy

The brief's preference order is honoured — real public data first, research-grounded semi-synthetic second, fully synthetic never — and the honest conclusion of this cycle's audits is a **three-layer strategy in which each layer does only the job it is qualified for.** No fully public construction order-level dataset supports the estimand. **[Verified absence]**

**Layer 1 — Semi-synthetic construction dataset: the narrative hero.**
- *Construction:* generated from a **fully published parametric data-generating process** — explicitly *not* built by transforming Olist records. The DGP's parameters (load distributions, late-rate bases, effect magnitudes and heterogeneity shapes, noise levels) are *calibrated to facts and estimates* from the Olist run and the published literature; no Olist microdata enters the files. This design choice is deliberate: it keeps the flagship dataset free of CC BY-NC-SA ShareAlike entanglement while remaining research-grounded rather than invented.
- *Content:* an order/supplier event log with supplier baseline capacity, concurrent backlog, material class and complexity, quantity, value, requested lead time, project urgency and phase, season, supplier selection, the **original committed milestone plus any revisions**, and actual completion — with confounding built in deliberately (complex packages route to specialized, loaded suppliers; priority influences both assignment and outcome) so that naive correlational analysis is *wrong by construction* and the demo can show the causal layer correcting it.
- *Ground truth:* every simulated order carries its true effect; heterogeneity varies by supplier capacity and material class. Five scenario variants are generated for the evaluation harness (§16).
- *Non-negotiable presentation rules:* every field carries a provenance tag (externally-calibrated / transformed / simulated); the DGP script is published in the repo; findings are never presented as facts about real construction projects; the deck states in one line why the layer exists ("no public construction order data exists; we validate the method where truth is known and run it unchanged where data is real").

**Layer 2 — Olist (real): the validation engine.**
- *Source & scale (audited):* 99,441 orders / 112,650 items / 3,095 sellers, Sep 2016–Sep 2018; 99.0% of seller-order lines carry the exposure clock, the promised supplier milestone (`shipping_limit_date`) and the actual supplier milestone (carrier handoff). **[Derived audit]**
- *Role:* the DML estimate, sensitivity summary, refuter battery, and clustered inference run here unchanged, presented as **out-of-domain real-world validation of the pipeline and mechanism** — never as a construction claim. Primary analysis restricted to single-seller orders (97.4% of lines) because the carrier timestamp is stored at order level.
- *License (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) — risk-managed position:* the use is student research/education in a competition; the dataset is not sold or embedded in a commercial product. Mitigations, all adopted: attribute Olist and the license wherever results appear; **never redistribute raw or transformed Olist files** — the repo links to Kaggle and ships a loader; keep the hero dataset microdata-free (Layer 1's parametric design); and present Olist results as a validation chapter rather than the submission's central evidence. The team decided not to pursue a separate written-permission request. This remains a risk-managed interpretation, not legal advice, and the design ensures the submission survives even if the Olist chapter has to be pulled.

**Layer 3 — SCMS (real): the rejection vignette.** Not a causal source (audit in §5). Its audit becomes an asset: one appendix slide — "we audited SCMS and rejected it: 89% of its scheduled dates in the analyzable subset are identical to the delivery dates, i.e., the outcome is administratively backfilled" — and one live demo beat where the eligibility gate fires on it. License: the official access-level note indicates CC-BY-with-attribution posting, but mirrors label it unknown; the raw file is not redistributed either way. **[Derived audit + Verified portal listing]**

**Unavailable for this effort — Kaya platform/sample data.** The official resources page offered access to registered teams on request, and the team completed that outreach. The team confirmed on 31 July 2026 that neither platform access nor a de-identified Kaya sample will be provided. Core therefore has no Kaya credentials, dataset, adapter, derived evidence, or live integration claim. The three published layers above are the committed data plan. Any later Kaya or contractor source belongs to a future effort and requires a fresh reviewed adapter and data-governance decision.

**Provenance principles.** One lineage record per number on screen: dataset, transformation, model version, assumption set. The evidence card's provenance entry reads directly from it.

**Path to real construction validation.** Post-hackathon: a retrospective run on one contractor's (or Kaya's) historical orders — re-estimate the ATTE and subgroup effects, re-run the full refutation/sensitivity suite, re-calibrate the product's numbers only then; the audit trail then accumulates the intervention-outcome data needed to graduate from exposure effects to measured intervention effects.

## 12. Causal Model and Scientific Validity

**Conceptual causal graph (described; research-backed and pre-specified, not practitioner-validated in this effort and not discovery-generated).** Nodes and edges: Supplier stable traits (capacity class, specialization, baseline reliability) → {Load, Slippage, Selection}; Market/season/calendar demand → {Load, Slippage}; Project urgency & phase → {Supplier selection, Requested lead time, Slippage}; Material category & complexity → {Selection, Load (via routing to specialists), Slippage}; Order quantity/value → {Load (attractiveness), Slippage}; **Load at placement (treatment) → production queue/prioritization → Slippage (outcome)** — the queue pathway is a mediator and is *not* adjusted for when estimating the total effect; Emerging slippage → escalation/expediting → final slippage (post-treatment; excluded). Automated discovery (PC/Tabu/Hill-Climbing, as in the anchor study) is run only as a *diagnostic cross-check* whose disagreements with the pre-specified DAG are shown, never as a source of causal truth.

**Identification assumptions, stated as the product will state them:** (i) *conditional exchangeability* — within the adjustment set, high-load and normal-load orders are comparable; made more plausible (never guaranteed) by the within-supplier, time-varying exposure and pre-placement supplier-history covariates; interrogated, not assumed, via sensitivity analysis; (ii) *positivity/overlap* — checked per order; out-of-overlap profiles route to abstention (propensity trimming, extremes flagged); (iii) *consistency* — the exposure is a standardized contrast at a pre-registered threshold with sensitivity across thresholds; the caveat that different open-order collections are different "versions" of high load is stated, and the estimand is described as a standardized exposure contrast; (iv) *interference* — formally strained, since orders sharing a congested supplier influence one another; handled by supplier-clustered inference, a leave-one-supplier-out stability range, a partial-interference stress scenario in the harness (Stretch), and an explicit user-facing limitation line (§13/§15). Time-varying confounding (projects reacting to earlier delays by changing later orders) is acknowledged; first-exposure analyses per supplier-project pair are used and longitudinal g-methods named as the correct future upgrade. Missingness and date-quality are profiled per run; informative missingness (struggling suppliers reporting worse) is a named sensitivity target.

**Estimators (exact classes, all verified current).** Primary: **DoubleML `DoubleMLIRM`** (v0.11.3) — binary treatment, flexible gradient-boosted nuisance models, repeated cross-fitting, propensity trimming, **score = ATTE** (trimmed-ATE run as context), supplier-clustered inference. Heterogeneity: **EconML `CausalForestDML`** (v0.16.0) for Stretch subgroup effects only; Core does not import or execute it. Refutation: **DoWhy** (v0.14) placebo-treatment, dummy-outcome, random-common-cause, and data-subset refuters, plus a negative-control outcome (a variable fixed before exposure — e.g., contracted unit price — whose "effect" must be ≈0). Comparison estimators run and shown as pedagogy and triangulation: naive difference-in-means, covariate OLS, normalized IPW ATTE, and supplier fixed-effects OLS. There is no separate AIPW comparator. If only the most complex model yields the headline result, the Evidence Verdict is Association Only.

**Sensitivity analysis and the Robustness Grade.** `DoubleMLIRM.sensitivity_analysis()` / `sensitivity_benchmark()` implement the omitted-variable-bias framework (Chernozhukov, Cinelli, Newey, Sharma & Syrgkanis), reporting bias bounds under hypothesized confounding strengths benchmarked against the manifest's reviewed observed-covariate groups. **[Verified by installation]** Rank groups by the lowest adjusted lower bound. *Strong* means the strongest benchmark's adjusted lower bound remains above zero; *Moderate* means the strongest does not but the adverse median does; *Weak* means the adverse median does not; *Unavailable* means no valid estimate or eligible benchmark exists. This grade measures hidden-confounding sensitivity only; overlap, balance, refuters, engine-repeat stability, and the Stretch leave-one-supplier-out analysis remain separate diagnostics. The card states plainly what sensitivity cannot do: repair a wrong graph, treatment measurement error, outcome leakage, or interference.

**Order-level CATE display boundary (Stretch only).** Core never computes or displays a CATE. A future contract must define the heterogeneity estimator, validation, local support, stability, and individualized-language policy; at minimum, the applicable Subject Verdict must be Supported under stated assumptions and the Robustness Grade Strong before any individualized causal effect can be shown. Until that contract exists, the deterministic message is: *"Individualized effect not shown — Core reports population evidence and subject applicability only."*

**Validation and falsification plan.** Covariate balance and overlap plots; the DoWhy refuter battery and negative control; threshold and specification sweeps (67th vs 75th percentile; minimum-history variants; continuous-load linear-slope sensitivity); time-period stability; supplier and category holdouts; on the semi-synthetic layer only — bias and interval coverage against known truth, plus the full five-scenario harness (§16). Subgroup-effect stability remains Stretch after a future heterogeneity contract. Versioned Diagnostic Results feed the Evidence Verdict policy and its separate Robustness Grade.

**Verdict triggers and precedence:** execution or integrity failure yields no verdict; upstream scientific ineligibility and an inconclusive primary interval yield Insufficient; a valid estimate with a causal-validity veto yields Association Only; otherwise a fragility trigger yields Tentative; otherwise the result is Supported under stated assumptions. The closed trigger registry, within-class ordering, and deterministic next steps are defined in the [validity verdict contract](specs/validity-verdict-evidence-abstention-contract.md); no renderer may invent a data deficit or collection duration.

**Limits on causal language (product-wide, enforced in generation):** Supported results use the exact estimated-increase template and retain “under the stated assumptions”; Tentative results say the possible increase is fragile; Association Only uses adjusted-association language with no causal verbs; Insufficient says the proposed driver is not supported. Prohibited: "the true cause," "causally supported," "definitely caused," "will save X days" absent an estimated intervention effect, and any forensic single-order attribution — an ATTE is never rendered as "the cause of this order's delay."

## 13. Decision and Intervention Strategy

**Generation.** Interventions come from a small, curated, governed library keyed to the supported driver — construction operating playbooks, contract/project constraints, and (in future) historical interventions — never from free-form LLM ideation. For congestion: secure a protected production slot; split the package across qualified sources; activate a prequalified alternate for remaining quantity; adjust release timing (proactive); phased delivery; re-sequence dependent site work; contractual escalation; accept-and-monitor.

**Evidence linkage — the exposure-vs-intervention gap, productized.** Every action card displays separate tags:
- **Driver evidence** (from the estimator): supported / tentative / association-only.
- **Mechanistic link:** the action plausibly changes the exposure (e.g., rerouting remaining quantity removes future congestion exposure) — but the action's own effect was not estimated.
- **Rule-based eligibility:** derived from specs, qualification status, contract terms, minimum-order rules, remaining float.
- **Assumption-based benefit:** projected benefit = *estimated exposure effect × declared recoverable fraction − action cost*, with the recoverable fraction shown as an editable assumption (e.g., "40%, assumption"), never as a learned result.
- **Speculative:** hidden by default behind disclosure.

The system never silently equates "congestion adds ~X days" with "switching recovers ~X days." Athey–Wager policy learning is *not* claimed for sourcing benefits — the available data contain no observed rerouting/expediting interventions with outcomes; a policy tree over "act vs monitor" is retained only as a Stretch illustration, labelled as decision-support illustration, never as an optimized sourcing policy.

**Driver-logic filtering (Core, and the demo's most quotable line).** When the supported driver is capacity congestion, a generic expedite request is logically weak — it asks a congested supplier to go faster. Expedite appears only when a real acceleration mechanism exists (overtime capacity, slot swap); otherwise the card explains the suppression. Constraint checks (qualified alternates, contract terms, float) prune the library before comparison; pruned options are listed with the binding constraint, preserving inspectability.

**Trade-off comparison.** Dimensions selected for this case: expected schedule protection (days, with interval and evidence tag), direct cost, time-to-initiate, contractual/relationship risk, disruption and reversibility, and evidence confidence. Cost of delay is a user-editable parameter (₹/day of critical-path slip) so the comparison is a transparent calculation.

**Selection and presentation.** After a Supported under stated assumptions verdict and recorded claim-scope permission make an option eligible for separate Decision Support: one dominant action → recommend exactly one, runner-up one tap away; genuine trade-offs → two options with the pivot stated ("choose on relationship cost"). Out-of-domain validation never enters this layer. Tentative routes to investigation; Association Only and Insufficient prohibit driver-linked recommendations. The manager can approve, edit, reject with reason, or request further analysis; every re-run and disagreement reason is logged (disagreement is treated as data — it may reveal missing contractual constraints, not model error).

**Artefact-layer safety (Core).** The structured evidence object contains every number, date, supplier name, spec, tag, and the allowed causal wording. Deterministic templates inject all of these; the LLM generates only connective prose, salutation, and tone between fenced slots. A post-generation checker extracts every numeral, date, percentage, entity, and causal-strength phrase from the draft and rejects any token absent from the evidence object's whitelist, any blocked action, and any wording or action stronger than the Evidence Verdict permits; on rejection it regenerates once, then falls back to the fully templated zero-LLM rendering — which also guarantees the live demo cannot hallucinate. Human preview and authorization are mandatory for every outbound artefact.

## 14. Conceptual System Architecture

Nine components, above implementation level; information flows strictly downward, and nothing reaches the user without a verdict from the validity service.

1. **Data & Lineage Layer** — ingests semi-synthetic, Olist, or SCMS records through one schema; computes the exposure index with expanding-history thresholds; enforces temporal eligibility (pre-placement covariates only; frozen promises); records provenance for every derived field. *Must not:* hide simulated or post-treatment fields.
2. **Risk-Signal Adapter (reactive) / Pre-Award Hook (proactive)** — receives a generic local risk event shaped to the publicly described Amber workflow or an award-time invocation; includes the prototype's deliberately ordinary gradient-boosted risk stub, which doubles as the prediction-only baseline. *Must not:* treat prediction as causality or imply a tested Kaya integration.
3. **Causal-Question Builder** — fixes treatment, outcome, estimand, comparison population, and assumptions; renders the question in plain language before analysis. *Must not:* let an LLM choose the causal story.
4. **Eligibility & Diagnostics Gate** — support, history, overlap, missingness, leakage, shift checks; hard-stops before estimation. *Must not:* produce an effect after failed eligibility.
5. **Causal Engine** — DoubleMLIRM ATTE (clustered), trimmed ATE, declared sensitivities, and comparison estimators. CausalForestDML subgroup effects remain Stretch and are absent from Core.
6. **Validity & Abstention Service** — refuter battery, negative control, sensitivity analysis and benchmark-relative Robustness Grade, stability checks; issues one of four Evidence Verdicts and owns abstention triggers. *Decision boundary:* the single chokepoint between analysis and user.
7. **Decision Layer** — governed intervention library, constraint filter, trade-off calculator, evidence tags; separates driver evidence from action assumptions.
8. **Explanation & Artefact Layer (LLM, fenced)** — verdict-first cards and drafted artefacts from structured inputs only, with the output checker and zero-LLM fallback.
9. **Manager Interaction & Audit** — approve/edit/reject/investigate loops; immutable audit trail of evidence snapshot + decision + realized outcome.

The predictive, causal, decision, and language components are separated precisely so a judge can be shown where correlation ends, where causal claims are policed, and where the LLM is fenced. The whole system runs **fully locally** (cached models and artifacts, zero-LLM fallback path) so the live demo has no network dependency — a robustness requirement given the verified live-demo format.

## 15. Product Trust, Transparency, and Failure Behaviour

The manager must always be able to inspect: the exact causal question and decision timestamp; treatment, outcome, and comparison-population definitions; the Evidence Verdict, its complete ordered triggers, and the separate Robustness Grade; the effect and interval in days when permitted; the adjustment set ("accounted for: material, size, value, season, lead-time buffer, supplier history") and the acknowledged gaps ("not observed: supplier internal shocks — an unmeasured confounder would need strength comparable to [named benchmark covariate] to overturn this"); alternative explanations considered (capable suppliers attract load; complex packages route to specialists; strategic promise-setting) — presented as the few most credible unresolved alternatives, not a generic disclaimer; the refutation scoreboard and negative-control result; data provenance and freshness; the assumption registry in plain language; each action's four tags; and every human edit and override.

**Out-of-distribution and invalid cases** (new supplier or category, order far outside history, failed timestamps, extreme propensities) produce registry-backed triggers with constructive next steps derived only from sanitized aggregate facts and exact numeric deficits. **Four Evidence Verdicts** govern everything user-facing: Supported under stated assumptions / Tentative — fragile / Association only / Insufficient evidence — abstain. Execution and integrity failures remain outside that vocabulary and produce no verdict. Every recommendation is overridable; every override is logged with a reason; and the audit trail replays exactly the evidence the manager saw at decision time — the accountability property that matters in construction disputes. Human authorization alone is not treated as sufficient governance: evidence shown, edits, approver identity, and later outcomes are all recorded.

## 16. Evaluation Framework

**Layer 1 — Causal validity.** On the semi-synthetic harness (truth known), the **five Core scenarios**, each mapped to a claim the product makes on stage: (1) *true effect* — ATTE bias within pre-registered tolerance and interval coverage ≈ nominal; (2) *null effect* — the system must report no supported driver (false causal-discovery rate); (3) *planted spurious correlate* — a variable engineered to predict slippage with zero true effect: the correlational baseline acts on it, the Copilot must refuse (negative-control rejection); (4) *hidden confounding* — the Robustness Grade must fall to Weak and the Evidence Verdict to Association Only; (5) *poor overlap* — abstention must fire off-support (abstention precision and coverage). **Stretch scenarios:** subgroup-effect rank quality after a future heterogeneity contract, treatment measurement error, dataset shift, and simplified supplier-level partial interference (one order's treatment perturbs the queue others experience — demonstrating that independent-unit estimates can bias, without claiming network estimation). On Olist (truth unknown): refuter, overlap, stability, and sensitivity results reported as-is; triangulation across estimators; no accuracy claims beyond what diagnostics support.

**Layer 2 — Decision quality.** Utility under the known DGP: delay-days recovered minus action, qualification, and disruption costs. Policies compared: **Copilot** (an option becomes eligible only when the Evidence Verdict is Supported under stated assumptions and separate driver logic and expected utility pass; execution remains human-authorized) vs **prediction-only** ("expedite whatever the risk model flags") vs **correlation-only** ("act on the top SHAP feature" — which the planted-correlate scenario is designed to embarrass) vs **always-expedite** vs **static load rule** ("avoid suppliers above a fixed historical-load threshold") vs the **oracle**. Reported: normalized regret vs oracle, unnecessary-action rate, harmful-action rate, preventable delay recovered, cost per day recovered, abstention quality, and performance under the adverse scenarios. The headline is not prediction accuracy; it is that the causal layer *selects better interventions* — and that if the simple load rule had matched the Copilot, the product thesis would be in trouble, which is exactly why the rule is in the table.

**Layer 3 — Manager usefulness and trust.** Human walkthrough validation is deferred outside this hackathon; no outside-participant evidence or comprehension score is claimed. The intended future protocol is a scripted walkthrough with 3–5 outside participants (ideally one practising PM or procurement engineer), using a think-aloud protocol to assess comprehension (restate driver, magnitude, uncertainty, and caveats after one viewing), inspectability (every on-screen number traceable in ≤2 interactions), calibrated reliance (recognize the unsupported action; override when appropriate; no blind acceptance), and control (no artefact leaves without approval). The target is calibrated reliance, not maximum trust.

**Layer 4 — Prototype and demo effectiveness.** The demo must land, in order: the real managerial problem; the causal contribution in one sentence; insight-to-action in one continuous flow; scientific credibility (visible refutation scoreboard, one live abstention, the sensitivity summary one click deep); originality (the narrow, disclosed claim of §19); scalability (the proactive trigger plus the second-driver preview); a memorable close.

**The prediction-only baseline, specified.** Same data, same pre-treatment features: the gradient-boosted risk stub with SHAP attributions. The judge-facing comparison: (i) on the harness, SHAP prominently ranks the planted correlate while the causal layer nullifies it — explanation vs causation, demonstrated not asserted; (ii) the regret table — decision value; (iii) side-by-side artefacts — a flag-plus-bars screen vs an evidence-cased recommendation. This comparison lives in the deck, video, and demo script; it is **not** built into the operational product UI, per the brief.

## 18. Prototype and Demo Narrative

## 19. Risks, Limitations, and Likely Judge Objections

*"DML doesn't prove causality."* Correct — and the product says so: it estimates a specified causal effect under explicit assumptions, tests observable implications, quantifies hidden-confounding fragility against named benchmarks, and abstains when credibility fails.

*"Your hero data is simulated."* By design, and disclosed field-by-field: no public construction order data exists [Verified absence]; the harness is the only way to *prove* (not assert) that the machinery recovers truth, refuses nulls, and rejects planted correlates — and the identical pipeline runs unchanged on 99k real orders in the validation chapter. A published DGP with ground truth is a stress-test environment, not a shortcut. Kaya platform and sample-data access were pursued but are unavailable for this effort.

*"Your real data is e-commerce, not construction."* Correct, and stated on the slide: same decision structure (supplier commitments, promised vs actual supplier milestones, within-supplier load variation), different commodity; the causal precedent is maritime ETO; the concentration mechanism is independently endorsed in construction-adjacent shipbuilding work; construction fit is carried by the disclosed hero layer; real construction validation is the named next step.

*"Isn't this the Wyrembek paper with a frontend?"* The anchor's treatment is a *static* multi-warehouse flag; ours is a time-varying within-supplier exposure with a leakage-safe threshold; we add supplier-controlled milestones, an eligibility/abstention gate, benchmark-relative sensitivity grading, the exposure-vs-intervention tag system, constraint-aware decision support, the artefact safety layer, and a ground-truth decision-quality evaluation. The paper is presented as evidence for the method-and-mechanism family, never as having estimated our exposure.

*"Kaya already flags risks, explains recommendations, and takes actions."* Acknowledged on the slide — the differentiation is *formal causal evidence and calibrated decision governance*, not the existence of explanations or actions; positioning is complement-and-extend, per the hackathon's own framing.

*"A simple rule — avoid loaded suppliers — gets most of the value."* The rule is in the regret table; on the harness it misfires exactly where effects are heterogeneous, which is demonstrable rather than rhetorical — and if it *had* matched the Copilot, the thesis would deserve to fail.

*"Supplier workload isn't observable in practice."* Often true; the product treats measurement quality as an eligibility input, displays it, tests alternative definitions, and abstains on poor measurement; capacity-slot confirmations are one of its own recommended actions.

*"Your recommended action was never causally evaluated."* Correct, and the card says so: driver evidence and action assumptions carry separate tags; projected benefits are exposure-effect × declared recoverable fraction − cost, with the fraction editable and labelled.

*"Orders interfere with one another."* Acknowledged as an estimand-level issue, not just an inference issue: clustered errors, leave-one-supplier-out stability, a partial-interference stress scenario, and the fixed limitation line — estimates describe the observed allocation environment.

*Originality (exact defensible wording, disclosure included):* "In a targeted search of English-language peer-reviewed and arXiv work, 2020 through July 2026, we found no prior study applying treatment-effect machine learning — DML or causal forests — to construction procurement or material-delivery delay. Related construction research uses predictive ML, optimization, or AI-based causal-attribution classification (e.g., JME 2026), and adjacent causal-ML applications exist in maritime manufacturing, rail, food delivery, and supply-chain finance — so we do not claim the first causal AI in construction. Our contribution is the combination of treatment-effect estimation, falsification, benchmark-graded sensitivity, hard abstention, and action-constrained decision support for construction material-delay decisions." Search absence is not proof of nonexistence; the wording carries its own scope.

*Ethics/operations:* a wrong reroute has real supplier-relationship and contract consequences — hence human authorization, assumption labelling, constraint filters, and the audit trail; systematically steering orders away from loaded suppliers could disadvantage smaller vendors at scale — named as a deployment-governance question. **[Inference]** *License:* Olist NC-SA ambiguity — mitigations in §11; the submission survives without the chapter. *Demo fragility:* fully local execution, cached artifacts, zero-LLM fallback, pre-rendered backups of every screen.

## 20. Adversarial Red-Team Critique

**R1 — "Your identification story is weaker than you admit: calendar-demand shocks and unobserved supplier stress raise both load and slippage; the within-supplier design does not close this."** *Verdict: partially conceded.* Response: calendar controls (month/season/holiday) enter the adjustment set; the Robustness Grade is promoted to the face of the card and benchmarked against reviewed observed-covariate groups; the negative-control outcome is mandatory; a supported headline retains the exact qualifier “under the stated assumptions” and displays the separate grade.

**R2 — "Recovering your own injected effect is circular; the harness proves nothing."** *Verdict: conceded in scope, answered in design.* Response: the harness is five scenarios, not one — null effect, planted correlate, hidden confounding, and poor overlap exist precisely so the system can *fail correctly* on stage; and the identical pipeline runs on real data where nothing was injected, with a pre-adjustment gradient (12.8% vs 7.1%) that existed before any modelling. **[Derived audit]**

**R3 — "The threshold manufactures the result."** *Verdict: partially conceded.* Response: the 67th-percentile expanding-history rule is pre-registered; 75th-percentile, alternative minimum-history, and continuous-load linear-slope sensitivities are reported; specification reversal is a causal-validity veto that limits the result to Association Only.

**R4 — "Exposure ≠ intervention; the reroute card is speculative for in-flight orders."** *Verdict: conceded and productized.* Response: reroute benefits apply to remaining/undelivered quantity and future orders only; recoverable fractions are numeric, editable, and labelled as assumptions; no learned policy claims anywhere.

**R5 — "The strongest decision moment is pre-award — before any risk flag exists — so a 'delay copilot' is mis-positioned."** *Verdict: partially conceded, absorbed.* Response: the dual-trigger design keeps the Stage 1 reactive promise as the lead while exposing the proactive check as the same engine invoked earlier; both triggers query one estimand; nothing forks.

**R6 — "Interference isn't an inference nuisance; moving one order changes another's outcome — the estimand itself shifts."** *Verdict: conceded technically.* Response: clustered inference and stability checks handle dependence, the partial-interference stress scenario demonstrates the bias mode, the limitation line states that estimates describe the observed allocation environment, and network estimation is named Future — no independence claims are made.

**R7 — "Managers won't read seven diagnostics; this is a research demo, not a product."** *Verdict: partially conceded.* Response: verdict-first progressive disclosure — one plain-language line and a separate Robustness Grade on top, everything else one tap deep; the verdict plus recorded claim scope governs whether separate Decision Support may evaluate an option, require investigation, prohibit causal interpretation, or abstain. The Layer-3 walkthrough study tests exactly this.

**R8 — "The LLM will eventually say something the evidence doesn't support."** *Verdict: conceded as a risk class, engineered out.* Response: template fencing with injected variables, the post-generation whitelist checker, single regeneration, then the zero-LLM fallback — which also de-risks the live demo.

**R9 — "The Kaya slide will get fact-checked by the people who built the platform."** *Verdict: conceded and resolved by verification.* Response: every positioning sentence traces to Kaya's own releases or the official hackathon pages; unverifiable feature names are banned; the gap claim is narrowed to formal causal evidence and governance; the framing is complement-and-extend, matching the hackathon's own words.

**R10 — "Why should anyone believe the 'first' claim?"** *Verdict: conceded.* Response: the broad claim is dead (JME 2026 exists and is disclosed); only the narrow, scope-stated wording of §19 survives, and it names its own nearest neighbours.

## 21. Revised and Hardened Final Recommendation

**What changed.** (1) The treatment's threshold became the leakage-safe expanding-history 67th percentile with a minimum-history requirement, and threshold sensitivity became mandatory (R3). (2) Calendar-demand controls and the benchmark-relative robustness grade moved to the card's face (R1). (3) The harness was fixed at five Core adverse-inclusive scenarios with the regret table's six policies (R2). (4) Recoverable-fraction assumptions became numeric, editable, and labelled; learned-policy claims were removed entirely (R4). (5) The dual-trigger design was locked with the reactive journey leading (R5). (6) The interference posture became explicit: clustered inference, stability range, stress scenario, fixed limitation line (R6). (7) The card became verdict-first with operational evidence states (R7). (8) The artefact checker and zero-LLM fallback joined Core (R8). (9) Kaya positioning was rebuilt on verified quotes only; PALS and the Lead-Time Index were banned; Jarvis references restricted to press-verified wording (R9). (10) Originality was narrowed to the disclosed, scope-stated sentence (R10).

**What was removed.** All "true cause / true effect / true driver" language; the static shared-supplier flag; the unexplained "+18.5%" visual; any construction effect-size assertion; SCMS as an evidentiary dataset; unverified Kaya feature names; learned sourcing-policy claims; conformal intervals from Core; promotional baseline screens inside the product.

**What was strengthened.** The identification design (within-supplier time-varying exposure, frozen promises, pre-treatment-only covariates, calendar controls, negative controls); the honesty architecture (four Evidence Verdicts, hard abstention, a separate benchmark-derived Robustness Grade, provenance, the SCMS rejection made into a feature); the value proof (adverse-scenario harness, six-policy regret, real-data gradient); the safety architecture (fenced artefacts, checker, local execution); and Stage 1 continuity (every deck claim now has a corrected, sourced counterpart).

**Which claims were narrowed.** Effect sizes: quoted only as the analogous maritime finding, never asserted for construction. Explanations: single-order forensic attribution is prohibited — an ATTE is population evidence about comparable exposed orders. Intervention value: assumption-labelled unless estimated. Scalability: a second driver requires its own DAG, treatment, covariates, and overlap test — the "one-click" beat re-runs a pre-specified estimand. Originality: the narrow disclosed wording only.

**Why this remains the strongest choice.** After attack, the proposal uniquely combines: peer-reviewed causal support for its exposure family; an audited real-data instantiation with a pre-existing gradient; a validation harness that proves the machinery on adverse cases, not just the happy path; a decision moment that is the fixed product identity, extended one step earlier without forking; a measurable beyond-prediction value claim; and a credibility posture — abstention, sensitivity, rejection of its own candidate dataset — that converts the judges' hardest questions into demo moments. No rejected candidate survives its own data-availability audit; the selected one survived two.
